Immersive login — redesign notes

Mock only. Compared against the current build (NanoLogin.tsx + LoginBackground.tsx), screenshotted on 3 Oct 2026 at 1440×900, 1366×768 and 390×844.

What's broken today

  1. Layers are cut off. The tree layer (E4) is cropped flat at the top of its band, so pine tips end in a straight horizontal line across the screen.
  2. Five art styles at once. A flat digital-blue sky, pasted cloud sprites, painted ridges, a photo-real eagle, fox and balloon, plus a CSS blur for the sun. It reads as a collage.
  3. Characters float. A goat stands in mid-sky, the owl hangs off the screen edge, and eagles fly across the logo.
  4. Clock collides with the scene. In daytime the time and date sit on top of the tree line; the date is unreadable.
  5. Night inputs fail contrast. At night the fields stay light grey with pale placeholder text on them, inside a dark card.
  6. Time of day barely shows. At 17:36 the sky is still noon blue.
  7. No single entrance. The clock shows before the scene and headline, the empty sky before the mountains, and the card before its fields.
  8. Cut-out fringes. Purple halos show around tree silhouettes.

What this design does

  • A lit valley. Hand-shaped peaks with a sunlit face and a shadow face that swap sides as the sun (or moon) crosses the sky. There's a rim light at golden hour, and the mountain bases fade into haze for depth.
  • A continuous 24-hour sky. Eight palette keyframes blend the sky, every layer, the snow, clouds, mist and water. The sun sets behind the ridges, light rays turn slowly by day, and the moon shows its real phase. Night adds stars, an aurora in the brand teal, fireflies and a rare shooting star.
  • Living details. Foreground pines and grass sway, mist drifts between ridges, clouds drift at three depths, and birds really flap. The lake mirrors the tree line and sky through an animated ripple filter, with a sun glint near sunset.
  • Readability first. The story text colour follows the sky's measured brightness, with a soft wash behind it. The clock stays inside the story column.
  • Adaptive glass card with correct contrast by day and night, plus "Keep me signed in" and a passkey option.
  • One GSAP timeline (~1.8 s) for the entrance: sky, ridges rising from far to near, pines growing in, headline, then the card and its fields. On success the camera pushes into the valley while the card lifts away (raced against 900 ms, like playExit()). An error shakes the card once.
  • Reduced motion pauses every loop and skips the entrance: you get a still painting.

How it would port

  • Replace PaintedEnvironment's WebP layers with a ValleyScene SVG component (generated once from a seed; colours from CSS variables).
  • Fold celestial.ts into the palette clock (the arc and lunar-phase maths carry over). GSAP is already a dependency.
  • Retire the anchor-dependent cast (Goat, Owl, Fox, Balloon, Dragon, Butterflies, loginCast.ts, anchors.ts).
  • Keep the existing contracts: playExit() with its 900 ms safety race, the null→error shake, and skipViewTransition.
