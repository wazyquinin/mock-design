Immersive login — redesign notes

Mock only. Compared against the current build (NanoLogin.tsx + LoginBackground.tsx), screenshotted on 3 Oct 2026 at 1440×900, 1366×768 and 390×844.

What's broken today

  1. Layers are cut off. The tree layer (E4) is cropped flat at the top of its band, so pine tips end in a straight horizontal line across the screen.
  2. Five art styles at once. A flat digital-blue sky, pasted cloud sprites, painted ridges, a photo-real eagle, fox and balloon, plus a CSS blur for the sun. It reads as a collage, not a place.
  3. Characters float. A goat stands in mid-sky between the headline and the card, the owl hangs off the screen edge with no tree, and eagles fly across the logo.
  4. Clock collides with the scene. In daytime the time and date sit on top of the tree line; the date is unreadable.
  5. Night inputs fail contrast. At night the fields stay light grey with pale placeholder text on them, inside a dark card.
  6. Time of day barely shows. At 17:36 the sky is still noon blue; golden hour and dusk don't really exist.
  7. No single entrance. The clock shows before the scene and headline, the empty sky shows before the mountains, and the card arrives before its fields.
  8. Cut-out fringes. Purple halos show around tree silhouettes, left over from background removal.
  9. The clock shows real time even when the scene is set to another time (?time=).

What this design does

  • One visual language. Five vector ridge layers in one flat-gouache style. Distance comes from colour and haze, not from different textures. Nothing is a raster cut-out, so nothing can be cropped or fringed, and all of it is tiny compared with the 0.4–1.7 MB of WebP layers.
  • A continuous 24-hour sky. Eight palette keyframes (night → dawn → morning → day → afternoon → golden → dusk → night) blend the sky, every ridge, snow, clouds and the lake at once. The sun and moon travel one arc and set behind the mountains. The moon phase is real.
  • Ambient life, not a zoo. A distant bird flock by day, stars, fireflies and a rare shooting star at night. No characters that need to be "anchored" to art.
  • Readability first. The story text colour is chosen from the sky's actual brightness, with a soft wash behind the column. The clock lives inside the story column, so it can never sit on the trees.
  • Adaptive glass card. Light glass by day and deep indigo glass by night, with inputs, placeholders, links and errors re-tokenised for both. It also adds the missing "Keep me signed in" and passkey options (the essential layout already supports both).
  • One entrance (~1.6 s). The sky fades in, the ridges rise from far to near, the headline words arrive, then the card and its fields. On success the camera eases toward the valley as the card lifts away; an error shakes the card once.
  • Reduced motion gives a still painting with no drifting, flapping, or entrance.

How it would port

  • Replace PaintedEnvironment's 10 WebP layers with one RidgeLayers SVG component (paths generated once with a seed, ridge colours from CSS variables).
  • Fold celestial.ts into a palette clock that writes the CSS variables (the sun/moon arc and lunar-phase maths carry over).
  • Retire the anchor-dependent cast (Goat, Owl, Fox, Balloon, Dragon, Butterflies, loginCast.ts, anchors.ts) and keep birds, stars, fireflies and the shooting star.
  • Keep the existing contracts: playExit() with its 900 ms safety race, the error shake on a null→error change, and skipViewTransition.
